Equations and details

The toy

Task A is 6 characters of the Omniglot alphabet Oriya, task B is 6 characters of Keble. Each character has 2 handwritten samples, and each sample has 3 extra copies shifted by one pixel, so each task has 48 training images. Every image is averaged down from 40 × 40 to 12 × 12 pixels, inverted (ink = 1) and divided by its largest pixel; this was precomputed from the same PNG files that the page shows. The 12 images on the right are the unshifted first sample of each character. They are training images: the toy measures what the network keeps, not how it generalizes.

Each column is an MLP: x (144) → h1 (16, tanh) → h2 (8, tanh) → one linear head of 6 logits per task. Adaptors of width 8 run from KB h1 into active h2 and from KB h2 into the active head. The network has about 5,400 parameters.

hiKB = σ(WiKB hi−1KB + biKB)
hiA = σ(WiA hi−1A + biA + αi ⊙ Ui σ(Vi hi−1KB + ci)), with σ = tanh and α initialized uniform in [0, 0.1]

The five segments

1. Progress on A. Cross-entropy of the active logits on the labels of A. It trains θA (active column and its head) and φ (adaptors); the KB is frozen at its random initialization.

2. Compress A. The teacher is a frozen copy of the whole network after step 1. The student is the KB backbone with a new KB head for A. Loss:

τ² KL(softmax(zT/τ) ‖ softmax(zKB/τ)), τ = 1, mean over the 48 images of A

There is no penalty here, because no earlier task exists.

3. Fisher. At the end of step 2, the diagonal empirical Fisher of the KB (backbone and head A) on the 48 images of A:

Fj = (1/N) Σn (∂ log p(yn | xn) / ∂θj)²

It estimates the curvature of the loss of task A at θ*, the KB after step 2. The running Fisher F*k = γ F*k−1 + Fk reduces to F* = FA, because A is the only earlier task. Then the active column, its head and the adaptors are re-initialized.

4. Progress on B. As step 1, on B. The KB is frozen at θ* and the new active column reads it through the adaptors.

5. Compress B. The teacher is a frozen copy after step 4. The student is the KB backbone (starting at θ*) with a new KB head for B. Loss:

KL(softmax(zT) ‖ softmax(zKB)) on the images of B + (λ/2) Σj F*j (θj − θ*j)²

The sum runs over the KB backbone (W1, b1, W2, b2). The heads are not penalized, and head A does not train: it reads task A from the moving backbone. The same code runs twice, with the slider's λ and with λ = 0. The KB never sees task A in this step.

The landscape and the springs

In segments 3 and 5 the right panel shows the loss of task A on a plane through θ*, the KB backbone after compress A, which sits at the origin. Direction 1 is the unit vector along θFT − θ*, where compress B ends without EWC. Direction 2 is the part of θEWC − θ* at right angles to direction 1, at unit length (Gram–Schmidt), so both end points lie in the plane. At λ = 0 the two runs are the same; direction 2 is then the direction in which that path bends away from direction 1 (the first principal component of its parts at right angles to direction 1). The plane is rebuilt when λ changes, and both axes have the same scale.

The lines are the loss of task A (cross-entropy on its 48 images, head A fixed) at θ* + a d1 + b d2, computed on a 67 × 59 grid; the shading is darker where the loss is lower. The springs are three of the backbone weights, picked by a fixed rule from the runs at λ = 0 and λ = 10 000: …. The thickness of a spring is proportional to λF*j, clamped between 0.7 and 4.5 pixels. The three tracks have the same scale, and the anchor is the value after compress A.

Optimizer

Full-batch gradient descent with fixed step counts: 200 progress steps at learning rate 0.5, 300 compress A steps and 600 compress B steps at learning rate 0.15, seed 2. In compress B, each step takes a gradient step on the KL and then takes the penalty part exactly (a proximal step): θj ← θ*j + (θj − θ*j) / (1 + η λ F*j). It has the same fixed points as gradient descent on the sum, and it stays stable for any λ. At λ = 0 the step is plain gradient descent. Everything is computed once at load and stored per step, so the scrubber shows recorded states. A new λ recomputes step 5 only.

Limits of the toy

The numbers come from one seeded run of a small network on 48 images per task. Seed 2 forgets the most at λ = 0 among seeds 1 to 6. The other seeds give the same trend: at λ = 0 the KB keeps 44 to 58% of task A, at λ = 5 000 to 10 000 it keeps 94 to 100% of A and learns 88 to 98% of B, and at λ = 1 000 000 it learns only 63 to 83% of B. With h2 = 16 units the KB forgets less (it keeps 48% of A at λ = 0) and a high λ costs less on B, so this toy uses 8. The real model of the paper is a convolutional network trained with minibatches over many more tasks.

Reference: J. Schwarz, J. Luketina, W. M. Czarnecki, A. Grabska-Barwinska, Y. W. Teh, R. Pascanu, R. Hadsell. Progress & Compress: A scalable framework for continual learning. ICML 2018. Glyphs: the Omniglot dataset (Lake et al., 2015).
